Reconstruction significantly transformed Southern society by ending slavery and expanding rights for African Americans. The 13th Amendment abolished slavery, while the 14th and 15th Amendments granted citizenship and voting rights. These changes helped formerly enslaved people gain more freedom and political participation.

However, Reconstruction did not completely transform the South because many white Southerners resisted these changes. Groups like the Ku Klux Klan used violence to limit Black political power, and Southern states passed laws that restricted African Americans’ rights. As a result, although Reconstruction brought important changes, its impact was limited by resistance and did not fully achieve equality.
